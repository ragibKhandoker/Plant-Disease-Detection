A Comparative Study of Plant Disease Detection Using Machine Learning

We compared deep learning models (a custom CNN and ResNet50 with transfer learning) against traditional machine learning models (Random Forest, AdaBoost and KNN) for automatic leaf-disease classification on the PlantVillage dataset.

Khandoker Md. Ragib Ahsan · Asif Al Mahfuz · Anika Afrin
Department of Computer Science and Engineering, BRAC University

Project overview

Plant diseases reduce crop yield and food quality around the world. Detecting them early and accurately helps farmers cut losses. This project trains five models on leaf images and compares them using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and confusion matrices.

Data preprocessing and augmentation

Basic steps

  • Image resizing
  • Normalisation (pixel / 255)
  • PCA dimensionality reduction for the classic ML models (150,528 → 100 features, 79.14% variance kept)

Augmentation

  • Horizontal flipping
  • Random rotation and zoom
  • Random cropping
  • Gaussian noise
  • Colour channel flipping

Why it matters

Augmentation exposes the model to more variety, which reduces overfitting and helps it handle real-world photos.

Models used

1. Custom CNN

Conv2D layers with Batch Normalization, MaxPooling, Dropout and GlobalAveragePooling, trained from scratch.

2. ResNet50

Transfer learning from ImageNet weights. Frozen backbone plus GlobalAveragePooling, Dense(256) and Dropout.

3. Random Forest

An ensemble of 100 decision trees trained on PCA-reduced features.

4. AdaBoost

Boosting with decision-tree stumps as weak learners.

5. KNN

K-Nearest Neighbors with k = 5 and Euclidean distance.

Results

ResNet50 with transfer learning clearly beat every other model. Traditional ML models struggled with raw pixel features, and AdaBoost performed worst.
